# Suspiciously Easy

### Finding the thing that makes you not just unique, but valuable

*A companion to the Secret Sauce test — and a way to do it on paper if you'd rather.*

**Draft 1** · 2026-09-10

---

## Before you start

There is a particular kind of person this book is written against.

They have read that they should "find their strengths." They have taken a
personality test and been told they are an Achiever, or a Blue, or an ENFP. They
have written *hardworking, passionate, detail-oriented* on an application form
and felt the words die on the page as they typed them. They suspect, correctly,
that none of this has told them anything they can use.

The problem isn't that they haven't tried hard enough. The problem is that
almost every tool available answers the wrong question.

Those tools measure **traits**. You are conscientious. You are extroverted. You
score highly on openness to experience. All of this may be true, and none of it
explains why anyone would choose you.

Nobody is hired, trusted, promoted, asked out, followed, or remembered for a
trait. People are valued for something much more specific: the overlap between
what comes easily to them and what is hard for everyone else. That overlap has
a shape. It can be named. And once it is named, you can point at it, spend it,
protect it, and ask to be paid for it.

That is what this book is about.

---

## What this book is, and isn't

It **is** a way to work out the specific thing you're for, using a method you
can check rather than a description you have to believe.

It **isn't** a personality test with better branding. There is no type here that
you *are*. There is a shape your answers make, an honest account of what that
shape is good for, an equally honest account of what it costs you, and the
words to describe it to someone who might care.

It is short on purpose. If a book about finding your advantage takes three
weekends, the book has become the obstacle. You should be able to read this in
an evening and finish with something you can say out loud.

## The two ways to use it

**The fast way.** Take the test at
[secret-sauce-eta.vercel.app](https://secret-sauce-eta.vercel.app) — it's free,
there's no signup, and nothing you answer is sent anywhere. Sixteen questions,
about five minutes. Then read Parts Three and Four, which are about what to do
with the result.

**The paper way.** Everything the test does is in here. Part Two contains all
twenty-four questions, the scoring, and the full catalogue of results. It takes
longer by hand and you'll be slightly less accurate, but you'll understand
exactly what's being measured, which has its own value. Appendix B walks you
through the arithmetic.

Either way, Part One is worth reading first. It's the part that explains why
you can't see your own advantage — and if you skip it, you'll answer the
questions as the person you'd like to be rather than the person you are, and
get a result that's flattering and useless.

---

## A warning about flattery

Every section of your result comes with a cost attached. This is deliberate,
and some people find it unpleasant.

The reason is simple: a description of you that contains nothing you'd rather
not hear is a horoscope. It will feel warm and change nothing. The parts of
this book that sting are the parts doing the work, and if you find yourself
arguing with one of them, that's worth more than the parts you nodded along to.

You are allowed to disagree. There's a chapter on how (Chapter 9), and it's
not a rhetorical gesture — disagreeing properly is part of the method.
